Five ways a network turns numbers into a decision. Each one below is running live. Change a setting and watch what moves.

Feedforward network and backpropagation

A small 2 → 6 → 1 network learns to separate two classes. This is a real network training on real gradients in your browser.

Dots are signals moving forward. Dark glow marks edges receiving large gradient.

What you are seeing

  • Forward pass. Each hidden node sums its weighted inputs and squashes the result with tanh. The output node does the same with a sigmoid to give a probability.
  • Edge colour. Blue edges carry positive weight, orange negative. Thickness is magnitude.
  • Backward pass. The error at the output is pushed back through each edge by the chain rule. Every weight moves a little against its gradient.
  • The map. Background shading is the network's belief at every point in the plane.

Try this: hover over the map to move the probe and watch node activations change. Switch to XOR and set the learning rate very low, then very high.

What you are seeing

  • Sliding window. The blue frame on the input is the patch the filter is looking at. The same nine weights are reused everywhere, which is why convolutions need so few parameters.
  • Feature map. Blue means the pattern was found, orange means the opposite pattern. With ReLU on, negatives are zeroed.
  • Pooling. Each 2×2 block keeps only its strongest response, so small shifts in the image barely change the result.

Try this: pick the horizontal filter on the Box image, then paint your own shape. Click the input to jump the window anywhere.

Recurrent networks

Words arrive one at a time. A hidden state is passed forward, carrying whatever the network chooses to remember.

What you are seeing

  • Each box is the same cell, reused at every step. Its six bars are the hidden state h: blue up, orange down.
  • Memory meter. For each step the page re-runs the sentence with the first word swapped out and measures how much h changed. That is how much the first word is still remembered.
  • Vanilla cells overwrite their state at every step, so early words fade quickly. The gated cell keeps most of the old state and only blends in new information.

Try this: flip between the two cell types and compare the meter at the last word. Type your own sentence of up to 10 words.

Self-attention

Every word looks at every other word and decides how much each one matters, then blends them into a new representation.

Attention matrix · row = reader, column = word being read

What you are seeing

  • Query. The highlighted word is asking a question. Arc thickness shows how strongly it attends to each other word, and the dashes flow toward the reader.
  • Softmax. Scores become weights that sum to 100%. Low temperature makes the choice sharp, high temperature spreads it out.
  • Output. The stacked bar is the recipe for the word's new vector: a weighted mix of the others.
  • Heads. A real transformer runs many heads at once, each learning a different relationship.

Try this: select it with head 1 and see which noun it resolves to. Then switch to head 2. These weights are hand-set to illustrate the idea, not learned.

Optimizers

Training is a walk downhill on a loss surface. Four walkers start from the same point on a curved valley. The minimum is at the cross.

OptimizerLossStepsStatus

What you are seeing

  • The surface. Darker means higher loss. The bands are contours. The valley is steep across and nearly flat along its floor.
  • SGD follows the raw gradient, so it bounces between the walls and crawls along the floor.
  • Momentum keeps a running velocity, so it rolls down the valley.
  • RMSprop and Adam divide each step by recent gradient size, so steep directions get smaller steps and flat ones get larger.

Try this: click anywhere on the surface to change the start. Push the learning rate up until SGD blows up while Adam still behaves.
