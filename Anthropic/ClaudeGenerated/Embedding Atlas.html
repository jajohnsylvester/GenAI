Step 1 · Lookup

Text goes in, numbers come out

The model first cuts text into tokens. Each token is swapped for a vector, one column of numbers. Type anything. Words this demo knows get a strong value on the topics they belong to.

Real models split text into sub-word pieces and use 768 to 3,072 learned dimensions that have no names. Here each of the 8 rows is labelled so you can read it.

Step 2 · Similarity

Close in meaning is close in angle

To compare two vectors, measure the angle between them. Cosine similarity is 1 when they point the same way, 0 when they are unrelated and −1 when they oppose. Drag the orange arrow. Its length changes nothing.

Step 3 · Structure

Directions carry meaning

The gap between two points is a direction, and a direction can mean something. The step from man to king is "add royalty". Take that same step from woman and you arrive at queen.

Step 4 · Retrieval

Search by meaning, not by keyword

Embed the query, embed every document once, then rank by cosine. A query like "a pet in the woods" shares no words with the best match, and it still wins. This is the core of semantic search and RAG.

Query as numbers

Each document vector is the average of its word vectors, and the query is treated the same way. Modern models read whole sentences with context, but the ranking step is identical.

Step 5 · Training

Where the numbers come from

Nobody types the vectors in. The model reads huge amounts of text and nudges words that appear in similar contexts toward each other, while pushing unrelated words apart. Press Run and watch the pull.

In practice

One idea, many jobs

Once meaning is a position, every question about meaning becomes a question about distance.

Semantic search

Find the closest documents to a query vector, even with no shared words.

Retrieval for chatbots

Fetch the most relevant passages and hand them to a language model as context.

Recommendations

Embed items and users. Suggest whatever sits nearest to what someone already likes.

Clustering and dedupe

Group tickets, reviews or articles by topic, and spot near-duplicates by distance.
