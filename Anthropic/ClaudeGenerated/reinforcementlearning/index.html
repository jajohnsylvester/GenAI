Learning by Trial & Error

Reinforcement learning (RL) teaches an agent to make good decisions — not by showing it answers, but by letting it try things and rewarding what works. Play with it below.

1The core loop

Everything in RL is one repeating conversation: the agent observes a state, acts, and the environment answers with a reward and a new state.

4The vocabulary

Hover (or tap) a card to flip it.

🤖Agent
The learner. Chooses actions to collect as much reward as possible.
🗺️State
What the agent currently sees — here, which cell it stands on.
🎮Action
A choice the agent can make: up, right, down or left.
🍬Reward
A number signalling how good that step was. The only teacher the agent has.
🧭Policy π
The agent's strategy: a mapping from state to action. Learning = improving π.
💎Value / Q
Expected total future reward from a state (or state + action). Not just the next reward.
⏳Discount γ
How much future rewards count. γ near 1 = patient, near 0 = short-sighted.
🎲Exploration ε
Chance of taking a random action to discover things, instead of the best-known one.
The one equation behind section 2

Q(s,a) ← Q(s,a) + α · [ r + γ · maxₐ′ Q(s′,a′) − Q(s,a) ]

“Nudge my old estimate toward what I just saw: the reward I got, plus the best I think I can do from where I landed.” Good news spreads backwards from ⭐ one step at a time — that's the green wave you see growing from the goal. Modern systems (games, robotics, RLHF for chatbots) replace the table with a neural network, but the idea is the same.
