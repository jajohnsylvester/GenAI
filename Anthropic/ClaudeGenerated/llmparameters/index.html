What is an LLM, and what can you configure?

A large language model writes text by predicting one small piece at a time. Below you can watch that happen, then change the settings that control it and see the odds move.

Two kinds of parameters

The word "parameters" means two different things. Mixing them up is the most common confusion.

Learned in training

Model parameters (weights)

Billions of numbers that hold what the model learned from huge amounts of text. They are fixed when you use the model. A "70B model" has about 70 billion of them.

  • You do not change them per request.
  • Bigger usually means more capable, slower and costlier.
Chosen by you, per request

Inference settings

Dials you send with each API call: temperature, top-p, top-k, max tokens, stop sequences, penalties, seed and more. They change how the next token is picked, not what the model knows.

  • This is what the playground below lets you explore.
  • The same prompt can give different answers with different settings.
Trained to predictDuring pretraining it reads text and learns to guess the next token. Later training teaches it to follow instructions.
Works in tokensText is cut into tokens, roughly word pieces. Limits, speed and price are all counted in tokens.
ProbabilisticIt does not look up an answer. It scores every possible next token, then one is sampled. That is why wording can vary.
