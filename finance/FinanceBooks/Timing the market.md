Timing the Market by Curtis M. Arnold
Chapter-Wise Summary & Indian Market Application
Part I: How to Profit from Technical Analysis

    Chapter 1: Two Roads to Success

        Concept: Fundamental vs. Technical Analysis.

        Indian Context: Balancing company earnings (Fundamental) with Nifty price action (Technical).

    Chapter 2: To Compute or Not to Compute

        Concept: Using software for charting.

        Indian Context: Utilizing platforms like TradingView, Zerodha Streak, or AmiBroker for NSE/BSE data.

    Chapter 3: Why Technical Analysis Works

        Concept: Market Psychology (Greed & Fear).

        Indian Context: Analyzing "Panic Selling" or "FOMO" rallies in the Indian retail investor segment.

Part II: The Basics of Technical Analysis

This section provides the "Toolkit" used for individual stocks (e.g., Reliance, TCS).

    Chapter 4: Technical Analysis and Charting

        Indicator: Bar and Candlestick charts.

    Chapter 5: Trends and Trendlines

        Indicator: Trendline slopes. Used to identify the long-term bull run of the Nifty 50.

    Chapter 6: Volume

        Indicator: Volume Bars. Crucial for identifying FII (Foreign Institutional Investor) activity on the NSE.

    Chapter 7: Reversal Patterns

        Indicators: Head and Shoulders, Double Tops/Bottoms.
    Shutterstock

    Chapter 8: Consolidation Patterns

        Indicators: Triangles, Flags, and Pennants. Often seen in Indian mid-cap stocks before a breakout.

    Chapter 9: Gaps

        Indicators: Breakaway and Exhaustion Gaps. Common in India during "Gap Up" openings following global market cues (like the NASDAQ).

    Chapter 10: Retracement Theory

        Indicator: Fibonacci Retracements. Used by Indian traders to find support levels (e.g., the 50% or 61.8% level) during a Nifty correction.

    Chapter 11: Support and Resistance

        Indicator: Horizontal Price Levels. Identifying "Round Number" psychology (e.g., Nifty at 20,000 or 25,000).

    Chapter 12: Momentum

        Indicators: RSI (Relative Strength Index) and ROC (Rate of Change). Used to see if a stock like Zomato or PayTM is "Overbought."

    Chapter 13: Moving Averages

        Indicators: 50-day and 200-day EMA/SMA. The "Golden Cross" on the Nifty is a major signal for Indian fund managers.

    Chapter 14: On-Balance Volume (OBV)

        Indicator: OBV Line. Used to detect if "Smart Money" is accumulating shares of an Indian company even if the price is sideways.

Part III: Stock Market (Broad Market Indicators)

This section focuses on the health of the entire Indian economy/market.

    Chapter 15: Dow Theory

        Application: Confirming a trend by comparing the Nifty 50 with the Nifty Bank. If one makes a new high but the other doesn't, it's a warning.

    Chapter 16: Breadth of Market Indicators

        Indicator: Advance-Decline (A/D) Ratio. Essential on the NSE to see if a rally is "broad-based" or just driven by HDFC Bank and Reliance.

    Chapter 17: Leadership and Quality

        Indicator: New Highs vs. New Lows. Tracking how many NSE stocks are hitting 52-week highs.

    Chapter 18: Market Sentiment Indicators

        Indicator: India VIX (Fear Gauge) and Put-Call Ratio (PCR). High VIX usually indicates a bottom is near in the Indian market.

    Chapter 19: Monetary Indicators

        Indicator: RBI Repo Rate and Inflation (CPI). Crucial for Bank Nifty movements.

    Chapter 20: Short-term Market Indicators

        Indicator: TRIN (Arms Index). Used by intraday traders on the NSE.

    Chapter 21: Industry Groups

        Indicator: Relative Strength (RS). Comparing Nifty IT vs. Nifty Auto to see which sector is leading the market.

    Chapter 22: Stock Options

        Indicator: Implied Volatility (IV) and Option Chain analysis (Open Interest).

Part IV: Cycles

    Chapter 23: The Meaning of Cycles

        Concept: Time-based rhythms.

    Chapter 24: Historical Stock-Market Cycles

        Indian Application: Tracking the 4-year Election Cycle and the "Budget Rally" (January/February) in India.

    Chapter 25: Practical Use of Cycles

        Strategy: Combining the Time (Cycle) with the Price (Indicators) to enter a trade with high probability.

Epilogue: Tomorrow’s Vistas

    Final Thought: Emphasizes that indicators are not crystal balls but tools for managing risk. Success in the Indian market requires the discipline to follow the indicators over personal bias.
