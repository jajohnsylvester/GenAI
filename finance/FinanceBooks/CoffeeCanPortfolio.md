  The book Coffee Can Investing: The Low-Risk Road to Stupendous Wealth, written by Saurabh Mukherjea, Rakshit Ranjan, and Pranab Uniyal, adapts a classic American investment philosophy to the Indian market.  

The core concept is to build a portfolio of high-quality companies and "forget" about them for at least a decade, allowing the power of compounding to generate exponential returns.  
Summary of Core Principles

The strategy is based on the "Coffee Can" idea pioneered by Robert Kirby, referring to a time when people hid valuables in coffee cans under mattresses.  

    Long-Term Horizon: The strategy requires holding stocks for at least 10 years without active trading or market timing.  

    Stringent Selection: Only businesses that consistently clear high financial bars are selected. This typically includes companies with a durable competitive moat and "clean" accounting practices.  

    Low Churn: By holding stocks for the long term, investors avoid frequent transaction costs and taxes, which significantly eat into overall wealth over time.  

    Quality First: The focus is on resilient, cash-generating businesses that have proven they can grow through various market cycles.  

Stock Selection Criteria (The "Screener" Rules)

For a stock to enter a Coffee Can Portfolio in the Indian context, it must meet specific historical performance standards:  

    Revenue Growth: At least 10% annual sales growth every year for the past 10 years.  

    Profitability (ROCE): Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) of at least 15% every year for the past 10 years.  

    Financial Health: Low leverage (debt) and strong free cash flow generation.  

    Market Cap: Typically focuses on established players (often large or mid-cap) with a market capitalization usually above ₹500 crores.
